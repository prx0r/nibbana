This is a substantial work to tackle. It is also an unusually good match for our previous readings of *Tantrasāra*, *Śiva Sūtras* and *Paramārthasāra*\: intensely subtle philosophy, strange paradoxes, extraordinarily precise terminology, and enormous consequences for meditation.

The source is Nibbāna - The Mind Stilled\. Ñāṇananda originally delivered these 33 sermons to forest monks at Nissaraṇa Vanaya in Sri Lanka between **12 August 1988 and 30 January 1991**\. They address the meaning of liberation in the earliest Buddhist discourses, frequently challenging received Theravāda interpretations. \[[Nibbāna – The Mind Stilled](https://seeingthroughthenet.net/albums/nibbana-the-mind-stilled/?utm_source=chatgpt.com); [Nibbāna: The Mind Stilled](https://www.buddhistinquiry.org/resources/lectures/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)\]

And there's an important discovery: **Bhikkhu Anālayo was directly involved in bringing these sermons into English.** He spent roughly ten years transcribing Ñāṇananda's English recordings and supplying textual references. He subsequently developed an entire 33-lecture course examining the sermons, including alternative translations and Chinese Āgama parallels. \[[The Nibbāna Interview](https://www.buddhistinquiry.org/article/the-nibbana-interview/?utm_source=chatgpt.com); [Nibbāna: The Mind Stilled](https://www.buddhistinquiry.org/resources/lectures/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)\]

That gives us three complementary levels of study: Ñāṇananda's original argument, the Pāli canonical passages behind it, and Anālayo's critical contextualisation.

We will preserve the precise order of all 33 sermons, rather than rearranging them into a generic guide to Buddhism.

## The complete ten-chapter architecture

I've mapped the 33 original sermons into ten consecutive sections. The natural turning point occurs after Sermon 26: Ñāṇananda shifts from untangling the conceptual difficulties of Nibbāna toward a particularly practical explanation of how the path is developed. \[[Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammàsambuddhassa](https://seeingthroughthenet.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Mind-Stilled_HTML.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com); [Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammàsambuddhassa](https://seeingthroughthenet.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Mind-Stilled_HTML.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com); [Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammàsambuddhassa](https://seeingthroughthenet.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Mind-Stilled_HTML.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com)\]

| Chapter | Original sermons | Our audiobook theme |
|--------|--------|--------|
| **I. The Fire That Went Nowhere** | 1-3 | Nibbāna, name-and-form, dependent arising, the whirlpool of existence |
| **II. The Cosmic Theatre of Bullshit** | 4-6 | Conditioned existence, mindfulness, and how conceptual constructions sustain the spectacle |
| **III. The Consciousness Paradox** | 7-9 | Non-manifestative consciousness, the problem of the world, and the limits of perception |
| **IV. The Mind's Hall of Mirrors** | 10-12 | Reflections, resistance, perception, and *papañca*, conceptual proliferation |
| **V. The Great ‘I Am’ Scam** | 13-15 | *Maññanā*, conceit, non-identification, and Bāhiya's devastating instruction |
| **VI. Liberation Without Somewhere Else** | 16-18 | The end of subject-object appropriation, the liberated mind, and crossing beyond becoming |
| **VII. Bliss Without a Buzz** | 19-21 | The two Nibbāna elements, happiness beyond feeling, and the untraceability of the liberated one |
| **VIII. The Questions That Self-Destruct** | 22-24 | The Tathāgata after death, the ocean simile, and reaching the world's end without travelling |
| **IX. Seeing Behind the Magic Show** | 25-28 | The Buddha's extraordinary knowledge, dependent experience, radical attention, and the four elements |
| **X. The Art of No Longer Chasing** | 29-33 | Gradual training, noble silence, crossing the flood, awakening factors, and craving as the cosmic seamstress |

These are our invented chapter titles, not titles in Ñāṇananda's original work. Each chapter follows its assigned sermons in order. \[[Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammàsambuddhassa](https://seeingthroughthenet.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Mind-Stilled_HTML.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com); [Barre Center for Buddhist Studies](https://www.buddhistinquiry.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Nibbana-03.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)\]

### How we'll maintain textual rigor

The primary text will be Ñāṇananda's complete [33-sermon text at Seeing Through the Net](https://seeingthroughthenet.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Mind-Stilled_HTML.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com)\. For each section, I'll cross-check the relevant [Anālayo course transcripts and lectures](https://www.buddhistinquiry.org/resources/lectures/), which add translation comparisons, canonical references and occasional Chinese parallels. These are especially valuable because Anālayo does not simply rubber-stamp every interpretation. \[[Nibbāna: The Mind Stilled](https://www.buddhistinquiry.org/resources/lectures/?utm_source=chatgpt.com); [The Nibbāna Sermons 1 to 11 by Bhikkhu K Ñāṇananda](https://www.buddhistinquiry.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Nibbana-01-1.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com); [Barre Center for Buddhist Studies](https://www.buddhistinquiry.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Nibbana-03.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)\]

The presentation will distinguish **canonical quotation**, **Ñāṇananda's interpretation**, **Anālayo's textual observations**, and **our ridiculous explanatory analogies**\. Pāli will be accurately transliterated, with morphological breakdowns rather than fictional etymologies. We will also keep the important disagreements with later Theravāda commentaries visible rather than pretending they never existed.

One essential philosophical safeguard: we will sometimes make illuminating comparisons with Trika, but never quietly turn Buddhist *anattā* into a doctrine of universal Śiva-consciousness. The points of convergence and disagreement are part of the fun.
